Article IV — Officers

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.1 Designation. The principal officers of the Association shall be a President, a Vice President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer, all of whom shall be elected by and from the Board of Directors. The Directors may appoint from the Board such other officers as in their judgment may be necessary or desirable. Two or more offices may be held by the same person, except that one person may not hold the offices of President and Secretary simultaneously.

Section 4.1 — Designation

The principal officers of the Association shall be a President, a Vice President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer, all of whom shall be elected by and from the Board of Directors. The Directors may appoint from the Board such other officers as may be necessary or desirable. When all Directors are seated, each Director shall hold no more than one office.

What Changed & Why

One office per Director. Officers still come from the board. What's new is that once every seat is filled, no Director holds more than one office. Washington law allows one person to hold several offices, as long as nobody is both President and Secretary (RCW 24.03A.585(3)). We chose to go further.

Volunteer boards tend to lean on whoever says yes. The person who can do more ends up doing more, until they burn out or step away and take what they know with them. One office each spreads the work evenly. It also means every Director learns one job thoroughly. Owning a real responsibility is how a new Director gets up to speed, and it's what makes a clean hand-off possible when the board changes.

"When all Directors are seated" keeps the board working through vacancies. Someone can cover a second office until the empty seat is filled.

Why these four offices? Each one covers work that can't be allowed to fall through the cracks: someone runs the meetings (President), someone can step in (Vice President), someone keeps the record (Secretary), and someone watches the money (Treasurer). State law requires a president, secretary, and treasurer (RCW 24.03A.585(1)). Other roles live in Section 4.8.

When there are fewer Directors than offices, the Board shall assign the responsibilities of each unfilled office to a seated Director.

Why: every responsibility has an owner, even on a small board. This goes with the additional positions in the proposal below.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.2 Election of Officers. The officers of the Association shall be elected annually by the Board immediately following the annual meeting, or at any other meeting of the Board held for such purpose, as set forth directly below. They shall hold office at the pleasure of the Board.

Section 4.2 — Election of Officers

The officers of the Association shall be elected annually by the Board at the next scheduled board meeting no more than 60 days after the annual meeting. They shall hold office at the pleasure of the Board.

What Changed & Why

A deadline for assigning officers. Before, officers were elected "immediately following the annual meeting," which gives newly elected Directors no time to get oriented, or "at any other meeting," which has no deadline at all. Now officers are elected at the next scheduled board meeting, and no later than 60 days after the annual meeting. Within two months of every election, each board responsibility has a named owner.

This timing was drafted together with an earlier draft of Article 2, Section 2.5 (Annual Meeting), where members elect Directors. Officer elections are the next step after that meeting.

Electing officers every year gives the board a regular point to rebalance roles. "At the pleasure of the Board" means the board can reassign an officer at any time by majority vote (see Section 4.3).

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.3 Removal of Officers. At any regular meeting of the Board or at any special meeting of the Board called for such purpose, upon an affirmative vote of a majority of the members of the Board, any officer may be removed, either with or without cause. A successor to the removed officer may be elected at any such meeting. Removal from office under this Section 4.3 shall not have the effect of removing the Director from the Board.

Section 4.3 — Removal of Officers

An officer may be removed, either with or without cause, at any regular meeting of the Board or at any special meeting of the Board called for such purpose, upon an affirmative vote of a majority of the members of the Board. At any meeting at which removal is considered, the board member or officer being considered for removal must be given a reasonable opportunity to speak before the vote. A successor to the removed officer may be elected at any such meeting. Removal from office under this Section 4.3 shall not have the effect of removing the Director from the Board.

What Changed & Why

A chance to be heard. The board can still remove an officer by majority vote, with or without cause. The new step is that the person being considered for removal gets a reasonable chance to speak before the vote. It costs a few minutes, and it means no one loses a role without the board hearing their side.

The last sentence isn't in our current bylaws. Removing someone from an office, such as Treasurer, doesn't remove them from the board. Members elected that person as a Director, and that is a separate decision (see Article III, Section 3.6). Spelling it out prevents anyone from reading an officer removal as a double penalty.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.4 President. The President shall preside at all meetings of the Association and of the Board and shall have all powers and duties usually vested in the office of the President, including such other duties as may be prescribed by the Board.

Section 4.4 — President

The President shall preside at all meetings of the Association and of the Board and shall have all powers and duties usually vested in the office of the President, including such other duties as may be prescribed by the Board.

What Changed & Why

No change. Every meeting needs one person responsible for running it: setting the agenda, keeping discussion on track, and making sure each item ends in a clear decision. Without that, meetings drift and decisions don't get made. The President does this at both board meetings and member meetings.

Washington law requires every nonprofit to have a president (RCW 24.03A.585). See Article II, Section 2.15 for who presides at member meetings.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.5 Vice President. The Vice President shall perform the duties of the President when the President is absent or unable to act, and shall perform such other duties as may be prescribed by the Board.

Section 4.5 — Vice President

The Vice President shall perform the duties of the President when the President is absent or unable to act or has a conflict of interest or any other reason, and shall perform such other duties as may be prescribed by the Board.

What Changed & Why

A backup for the chair, including during conflicts. The Vice President keeps things moving when the President can't run a meeting, so the board doesn't stall or have to pick a chair on the spot.

New: the Vice President also steps in when the President has a conflict of interest. If an agenda item affects the President personally, someone else should run that discussion (see Section 4.10). Naming the Vice President in advance settles who that is.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.6 Secretary. The Secretary shall keep the minutes of all meetings of the Board and of the Association and shall have custody of the business records of the Board and the Association, other than financial records kept by the Treasurer. The Secretary shall also perform such other duties as may be prescribed by the Board.

Section 4.6 — Secretary

The Secretary shall keep the minutes of all meetings of the Board and of the Association and shall have custody of the business records of the Board and the Association, other than financial records kept by the Treasurer.

What Changed & Why

The keeper of the record. Minutes are the official record of what the board and members decided. If nobody owns them, decisions get misremembered, disputed, or lost when the board changes. The Secretary keeps the minutes and the association's non-financial records, so there is one place to look and one person accountable for it.

Washington law requires every nonprofit to have a secretary, and the same person can't be both President and Secretary (RCW 24.03A.585). The person running the meeting shouldn't also be the only one writing down what happened. The closing "other duties" sentence was dropped as redundant: the board can still assign duties under Section 4.8. See Article II, Section 2.15 for recording at member meetings.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.7 Treasurer. The Treasurer shall have responsibility for Association funds and securities, shall be responsible for keeping full and accurate accounts of all receipts and disbursements in books belonging to the Association, and shall be responsible for the preparation of all required financial statements, audits, and tax returns.

Section 4.7 — Treasurer

The Treasurer shall have responsibility for ensuring Association funds and securities are deposited in federally insured depositories, shall be responsible for keeping full and accurate accounts of all receipts and disbursements in books belonging to the Association, shall be responsible for preparing all required financial statements, audits, and tax returns, including financial reports presented to the board at least quarterly.

What Changed & Why

Someone watching the money. Association funds belong to every member. The managing agent handles day-to-day bookkeeping, and the Treasurer is the Director accountable for checking that work. Washington law requires every nonprofit to have a treasurer (RCW 24.03A.585).

Two additions. First, association money must be kept in federally insured accounts, so it is protected if a bank fails. Second, the board gets a financial report at least every quarter. That way problems come to light within months, not at year-end.

New Proposal: Additional Positions

Board members change. The work doesn't. Officer positions put one named person in charge of each job, because when something is everybody's job, it ends up being nobody's job.

Picture this: what if we had no Secretary, and recording was assigned to all seven board members? Who would write the minutes? Who would make sure members had access to records? What if there were no Treasurer? Would the board have to vote every time we needed a bank statement? And without a President, would seven co-chairs run one meeting?

Officer positions are essential, and these four are the foundation of every organization. Our organization has more work than that. The essential addition is a Contracts Officer, so that every contract is in one place: our independent contractors for security and common areas, our managing agent, and service contracts like street sweeping.

This belongs in the bylaws. The bylaws set the structure of the organization, so this is where we build the net that keeps anything from falling through the gaps as the board has natural turnover.

Key Proposal — Contracts Officer

The Contracts Officer shall be responsible for oversight of all Association contracts, including those with the managing agent, independent contractors, and service providers; for ensuring that each party's obligations are fulfilled; and for recommending to the Board when a contract should be renewed, renegotiated, or ended.

Why This Matters

Bringing every contract into one place for oversight protects the association's assets. For example, the T-Mobile account, which we kept paying for months after we no longer needed it, would be this officer's responsibility.

Day to day
  • Tracks contract fulfillment for the managing agent and contractors: security, common areas, street sweeping, and service accounts
  • Makes recommendations to the board
  • Supports the Finance Committee

In addition to the Contracts Officer, we work with members and volunteers and care for many properties. Our organization would benefit from officers who are directly responsible for each of these areas and can take ownership of them.

Four more roles

Proposed — Communications Director

The Communications Director shall be responsible for the Association's communications with Members, including the accuracy, timeliness, and accessibility of information the Association publishes, and for recommending communication policies to the Board.

Why This Matters

This complements the Secretary by splitting a large job in two. The Secretary focuses on the board, and this role faces outward to members.

Day to day
  • Keeps the communications calendar and sorts incoming information
  • Writes or approves explainers for the website and newsletter
  • Works with the clerk on website resources
  • Writes communication policies for board approval
  • Supports the Tech and Events committees

Proposed — Member and Volunteer Advocate

The Member and Volunteer Advocate shall be responsible for recruiting, coordinating, and supporting the Association's volunteers; for serving as a point of contact to whom Members may bring questions and concerns; and for tracking issues commonly raised by Members and reporting them to the Board.

Why This Matters

Members need someone they can talk to and feel safe with, and that trust is what brings people into volunteering. For the board, this role keeps volunteers engaged and supported so committee work doesn't depend on the same few people. Over time, it builds the pipeline of members ready for committees and, eventually, the board.

Day to day
  • Connects members to resources and tracks common issues
  • Recruits volunteers and brings members into committees
  • Supports the Safety & Security and Governance committees

Proposed — Governance Administrator

The Governance Administrator shall be responsible for monitoring the Association's compliance with applicable statutes, the Covenants, these Bylaws, and Board policies; for recommending changes that keep the Association's governance transparent and fair; and for serving as liaison to other homeowner and neighborhood associations.

Why This Matters

We want governance that is predictable and fair to members, and that intention shouldn't fade or shift depending on who is on the board.

Day to day
  • Tracks RCWs, bylaws, covenants, and policies
  • Recommends changes for transparency and fairness
  • Works with other HOA groups and neighborhood associations
  • Supports the Governance Committee

Proposed — Common Areas Director

The Common Areas Director shall be responsible for oversight of the Association's common areas, including tracking their condition and needs, and for serving as the Board's liaison to the Common Area Manager, the City of Olympia, and the committees that advise on common areas.

Why This Matters

The board is ultimately responsible for common areas, and a dedicated board volunteer doesn't have the limits a Common Area Manager does. It's a large job with many stakeholders, and they all deserve a voice on the board.

Day to day
  • Tracks needs and updates for common areas
  • Works with the Common Area Manager, advisory committees, and the City of Olympia
  • Supports the Lake, Urban Forest, LRP, and Garden committees

How This Fits a Board of 3, 5, or 7

Proposed addition to Section 4.1: “When there are fewer Directors than offices, the Board shall assign the responsibilities of each unfilled office to a seated Director.” Every responsibility has an owner, even on a small board.

Alternative: instead of naming each role in the bylaws, “The board shall maintain a roles policy which provides offices and specific responsibilities to all remaining board seats.” This can simplify the bylaws, but it relies on policy to do structural work.

“Why This Matters” and “Day to day” describe the goal of each role. They are not part of the proposed language.

Section 4.8 Other Officers and Employees. Other officers of the Association and any persons employed to assist the officers, shall have such authority and shall perform such duties as the Board may prescribe within the provisions of the applicable statutes, the Declaration, and these Bylaws.

Section 4.8 — Other Officers and Employees

The board shall have the authority to appoint any persons to assist the officers and perform such duties as needed within the statutory provisions, the Covenants, and the Bylaws.

What Changed & Why

Help for the officers. The board can bring in people to assist the officers, as long as their work stays within the law, the Covenants, and these Bylaws. Officers now come only from the board (Section 4.1), so "other officers" was dropped from this section.

"Declaration" is now "Covenants," the term members use. The new officer positions are in the proposal above.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.9 Compensation. No Director shall receive compensation for serving as a Director but may be reimbursed for actual and reasonable expenses incurred in connection with administration of the affairs of the Association.

Section 4.9 — Compensation

No Director shall receive compensation for serving as a Director but may be reimbursed for actual and reasonable expenses incurred in connection with administration of the affairs of the Association with Board approval.

What Changed & Why

Volunteers, not employees. Directors can't be paid for serving, so no one can vote themselves a salary out of member dues. They can be reimbursed for real expenses, so volunteering doesn't cost anyone money out of pocket.

New: reimbursements need board approval. Every payment to a Director is approved in the open and shows up in the minutes. Officers are always Directors (Section 4.1), so this rule covers them too.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 4.10 Conflicts of Interest. This Section 4.10 will apply if and only if the Articles are amended such that this paragraph would not conflict with the Articles; however, nothing in the Articles or this paragraph will be construed as permitting Director behavior that would conflict with the Act or other applicable law with respect to duties owed by Directors. Nothing in the Association's governing documents shall be construed to authorize the Association or the Board to enter into any contract, employment, or other transaction between the Association and one or more of its Directors or any other corporation, firm, association, or entity in which one or more of its Directors are directors or officers or are financially interested. Any such contract, employment, or transaction shall be void unless, after the fact of such relationship or interest is disclosed or known to all of the Owners entitled to vote, such contract, employment, or transaction has been authorized or approved by Owners holding a majority of the voting power of the Association, excluding any interested Directors and the votes of the Units of which they are Owners, and the contract, employment, or transaction is fair and reasonable to the Association. Directors shall disclose any conflict of interest they may have and shall refrain from voting on any matter for which such a conflict of interest exists.

Section 4.10 — Conflicts of Interest

Nothing in the Association's governing documents shall be construed to authorize the Association or the Board to enter into any contract, employment, or other transaction between the Association and one or more of its Directors or any other corporation, firm, association, or entity in which one or more of its Directors are directors or officers or are financially interested.

Any such contract, employment, or other transaction shall be void unless, after the fact of such relationship or interest is disclosed or known to all of the Members entitled to vote, such contract, employment, or transaction has been authorized or approved by Members holding a majority of the voting power of the Association, excluding any interested Directors and the votes of the lots of which they are Members, and the contract, employment, or transaction is fair and reasonable to the Association. Directors shall disclose any conflict of interest they may have and shall refrain from voting on any matter for which such a conflict of interest exists.

What Changed & Why

The inoperative clause was removed. The Sound Legal draft opened with a condition: this section would apply only if the Articles of Incorporation were first amended so they no longer conflicted with it. Until then, the association's strongest conflict-of-interest protections would stay switched off. We are amending the Articles as part of this revision, so the condition isn't needed. Conflicts of interest are a real problem that small HOA boards face, so these protections need to be robust and foundational.

What it does: Directors must disclose conflicts and sit out the vote. A contract with a Director, or with a business a Director is involved in, is void unless members approve it with the conflicted Directors' votes excluded, and the deal is fair to the association. That's stricter than our current Articles of Incorporation (Article X), which only require the interest to be disclosed to the board. That is the conflict the amendment fixes.

"Owners" and "Units" are now "Members" and "lots," matching the rest of these bylaws.

Conflicts of interests include, but are not limited to, contracts, awards, or discussion directly affecting the lot or property of a director.

Why: defining a conflict in the bylaws leaves fewer questions about what counts.
