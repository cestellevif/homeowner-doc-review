New here? Start with the bylaws

Our bylaws were written in 2008. Washington State has a new law for homeowners' associations, and by January 1, 2028, every HOA has to follow it. Where our bylaws don't match the new law, the law wins.

To get started, the board asked the Association's attorneys, Sound Legal, to draft an initial set of updated bylaws. That draft is what the committee is working from now, reviewing and revising it section by section.

In the coming year, members will vote on revised bylaws. This site walks through the draft one section at a time, so you can see what's changing, why, and tell the committee what you think.

What bylaws are

Think of the bylaws as our community's constitution: the trunk of the tree, strong and hard to move. Everything else in the HOA grows out of them. They set how the board is elected, how meetings work, and most of all, how members participate. That's why changing them takes a vote of the members.

How our documents fit together

  3. 3 · Bylaws

  4. 4 · Policies

    Day-to-day details set by the board. The board can change them at any board meeting.

If two of these ever conflict, the one higher on the list wins.

Bylaw or policy?

Both are rules, but they work differently. Bylaws are approved by members, and changing them takes a member vote. Policies are set by the board and can change at any board meeting.

Day-to-day details, like park hours or how to update your email address with the HOA, fit well in a policy. Anything that protects your right to see, speak, or decide belongs in the bylaws. So does the basic setup of the HOA, like who the officers are and what they do.

An example: state law says our HOA must have a treasurer, but leaves the treasurer's job up to us. If that job were set only by board policy, one board could make the treasurer responsible for the money and the budget, and the next could make it about bringing snacks to meetings. That's why the important jobs are spelled out in the bylaws, where only a member vote can change them. Clear bylaws also save money: vague rules lead to disagreements, and legal bills come out of our dues.

A simple way to read: the FAIR test

Reading a whole set of bylaws at once is a lot. A simpler way in is to check each piece with four questions:

F

Find out. Does it protect your right to know what's happening?

A

Ask. Does it protect your right to be heard?

I

In the vote. Does it protect your right to decide?

R

Repair. Does it give us a way to fix things when they go wrong?

If a protection you care about lives only in a policy, or nowhere at all, FAIR helps you spot it.

How to read a section

Each section of the revised bylaws is a card like this one. Try clicking the yellow tab on the left.

Sound Legal — Original

Section 3.10 Regular Meetings. Regular meetings of the Board of Directors shall be held at such place, day and hour as the Board from time to time may specify by resolution made available to Owners. Subject to any applicable notice requirements, there shall be at least one (1) regular Board meeting each fiscal quarter.

Example — Section 3.10 Regular Meetings

Regular meetings of the Board of Directors shall be held at such place, day, and time as the Board specifies to the Members. There shall be at least one regular Board meeting each calendar quarter and at least six regular board meetings each calendar year.

What Changed & Why

The board must hold at least six regular meetings a year, the minimum needed to keep the Association's business moving.

Words to know

HOA (homeowners' association)
The group every lot owner belongs to. Ours is legally named Lakemoor Community Club.
RCW (Revised Code of Washington)
The book of all Washington State laws. RCW 64.90 is the new chapter for HOAs like ours, and it applies to every Washington HOA starting January 1, 2028.
Bylaws
The rules for how our HOA runs: elections, meetings, voting, and members' rights.
Covenants (CC&Rs)
Promises tied to every lot, recorded with the county. They apply to every owner, now and in the future.
Policies
Day-to-day details set by the board. Easy to change.
